How we reach a conclusion

Evidence has boundaries.

Each verdict applies to the exact claim shown. The evidence type tells you how we know; the limitations explain where that knowledge ends.

01

Define the claim

Separate the existence of a tool from claims about who built it, how it was used, and how many people were affected.

02

Read the underlying record

Prefer court opinions, official documents, vendor documentation and original leaked material. A publisher's interpretation is assessed separately.

03

State the limits

A proposal is not a working tool. A working tool is not proof of deployment. Deployment is not proof of universal surveillance.

What the verdicts and evidence labels mean

Supported: the reviewed evidence supports the specific, limited claim. It is not a guarantee that every detail or present-day implication is known.

Not established: the reviewed evidence is insufficient for the claim. This does not mean the claim has been proved false.

Court record / official publication: an attributable institutional record. Its authority does not make every interpretation beyond challenge.

Vendor documentation: first-party descriptions of a product. Useful for architecture; not an independent audit of all possible behavior.

Leaked document: an underlying document published outside official channels. We identify its provenance and distinguish descriptions from confirmed deployment. This review does not independently authenticate every document.

Evidence gap: the source set does not establish the claim. The listed sources explain adjacent facts and the limits of the inference; they are not evidence for a universal negative.

Review scope: this is a selected public-record review focused on U.S. surveillance, with a U.K. collaboration example. It is not an exhaustive intelligence history, a current legal guide, or a probability estimate that a reader is being monitored. Historical dates are intentional.

A little less jargon

Terms, in plain language.

Air gap
Separation from other networks. An added communication path can defeat that separation.
Backdoor
A hidden or unintended way to bypass normal access controls. A privileged component is not, by its existence alone, proof of a deliberate backdoor.
Endpoint
The device where information is created, displayed or used, such as a phone or laptop.
Exploit
A method that takes advantage of a weakness in software or hardware.
Firmware
Software closely tied to hardware, often involved in starting or controlling a device.
Implant
Software or hardware placed in a system to provide covert access or collection.
Selector
An identifier, such as an email address, used to specify a collection target.
Zero-day
A vulnerability not yet addressed by an available fix, or an exploit using such a vulnerability; usage varies with context.
ANT catalog
A leaked collection of descriptions of surveillance implants. A catalog entry is not a deployment log.
Vault 7
A series of CIA-related documents published by WikiLeaks in 2017. Assess the underlying document separately from publisher commentary.
